01 · Introduction

Problem Statement & Research Scope

The rapid proliferation of Fifth Generation (5G) mobile network infrastructure has fundamentally altered the threat landscape for telecommunications security. As 5G assumes a load-bearing role across latency-sensitive applications — autonomous vehicular systems, industrial automation, smart city deployments — network reliability and the timely detection of anomalous behaviour become imperatives of critical importance.

Anomalous conditions in 5G traffic may manifest as security intrusions, performance degradation, or hardware failures. The protocol heterogeneity and pervasive end-to-end encryption characteristic of 5G environments render real-time detection substantially more difficult than in preceding generations.

This work presents a systematic evaluation of multiple anomaly detection paradigms applied to 5G traffic: classical supervised and unsupervised ML, deep residual network architectures, sequence-based n-gram modelling, GAN-based data augmentation, and a federated learning framework enabling collaborative detection without centralised data exposure. Experiments are conducted on two publicly available 5G traffic corpora — 5G-NIDD and 5G-PFCP.

02 · Datasets

Two Complementary 5G Traffic Corpora

5G-NIDD

Predominantly TCP, HTTP, DNS, and QUIC traffic. Class distribution: 75.7% benign / 24.3% malicious. SMOTE applied during training to mitigate majority-class bias.

5G-PFCP

Richer protocol mix: SCTP, NGAP, HTTP/2, ICMPv6, ARP, MDNS. Near-balanced distribution: 53.6% benign / 46.4% malicious.

Together, NIDD stress-tests robustness under class imbalance while PFCP provides a near-balanced reference, spanning a broad spectrum of protocol behaviours and attack vectors.

Key observation

CDF analysis of inter-arrival times reveals statistically distinguishable signatures between benign and malicious traffic. These features encode anomaly-discriminating information independently of payload content — particularly significant for encrypted 5G traffic where deep packet inspection is infeasible.

Feature engineering

Features are extracted at the session level (5–10 min windows) across four categories:

  • Temporal features — per-pair inter-arrival times (mean, median, std), burst count, mean & max burst length
  • Packet-size statistics — total, incoming, outgoing counts; mean, std, median per direction; size & direction of first 10 packets
  • Protocol & header fields — port numbers, protocol ID, control flags (SYN, ACK…)
  • Timestamp-derived features — hour, minute, second encoding diurnal and sub-minute patterns

04 · Evaluation Protocol

Three Experimental Scenarios

Models are evaluated under three dataset-construction scenarios via k-fold cross-validation (k = 3).

  • Scenario A — Benign-only training. Test set has unseen attacks. Highest memory overhead (3,072 MB). Evaluates zero-day generalisation.
  • Scenario B — One attack type. Training includes one known attack class + benign. Longest runtime (4,215 s), reflecting cross-distribution difficulty.
  • Scenario C — Four attack types. Best runtime–memory trade-off; closest approximation to production deployment.

07 · Deep Learning

ResNet18 for Network Flow Classification

ResNet18 is adapted for traffic classification with 18 layers across four stages, each containing two convolutional blocks with batch normalisation and ReLU. Input encodes M packet sizes and transmission directions per flow. Optimised with Adam + categorical cross-entropy; hyperparameters selected via grid search with cross-validation.

08 · Federated Learning

Privacy-Preserving Distributed Detection

The federated learning system is a client-server architecture in which participating network nodes independently train local autoencoder models on private traffic data and transmit only model parameter updates to a central aggregation server. At no point is raw traffic data centralised, rendering this approach applicable to privacy-sensitive telecommunications infrastructure subject to regulatory constraints. Integrating ResNet into the federated pipeline (ResNet-Enhanced variant) improves F1-score from 89.9% to 93.2%, at the cost of an increase in training time from 2.3 to 3.8 hours per round.

09 · Sequence Analysis

N-gram Sequence Modelling

N-gram analysis captures temporal dependencies in packet sequences that aggregate flow-level statistics cannot represent. During training, all n-grams observed in normal traffic are catalogued within a space-efficient Bloom filter. At inference, each incoming packet sequence is assigned an anomaly score proportional to the fraction of constituent n-grams absent from the training catalogue; sequences exceeding a configurable threshold are classified as malicious.

12 · Conclusions

Conclusions & Future Work

Multi-algorithm framework. Random Forest achieves an F1-score of 95.01% under controlled conditions and 84.74% generalising to novel attacks; the federated ResNet-enhanced autoencoder attains 93.2%, demonstrating superior generalisation relative to the baseline federated model.

Synthetic data augmentation. The GAN reproduces statistical properties of real 5G traffic within a maximum Shannon entropy deviation of ±3.25%, confirming suitability for augmentation without degrading training integrity.

Privacy-preserving detection. The federated framework eliminates raw data centralisation and reduces membership-inference attack success from 78.3% to 12.7% — a reduction of 83.8 percentage points — while lowering per-node energy from 1.43 to 0.35 kWh.

Feature importance. Temporal patterns (37.2%) and packet-size distributions (24.8%) are the primary discriminative drivers, confirming meaningful detection is achievable from metadata-level features in encrypted environments.

Limitations & future directions

  • Encrypted payload opacity contributes 16.0% of misclassifications; session-level behavioural analysis and side-channel timing are promising remediations.
  • Computational overhead — the federated ResNet-enhanced variant needs 4.8 training hours and 3.7 GB peak memory per node, potentially exceeding constrained edge elements.
  • Low-volume evasion accounts for 28.9% of false negatives; more sophisticated sequence-level analysis and adaptive thresholds are required.
  • Future work spans personalised and asynchronous federated protocols, explainability-aware model design, cross-domain knowledge transfer, and extension to emerging 6G architectures.
